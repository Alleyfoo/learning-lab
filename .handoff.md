   to propose_rule (routing intelligence unchanged -- S15 changes the OUTCOME of
   a misroute, not its rate). The mandatory gate caught both and demoted them to
   DUPLICATE_RULE before the conflict gate could wave them through. S14 3/6
   wrongly ACTIVE -> S15 0/6 ACTIVE.
2. **The positive control holds.** new_rule 6/6 still reaches ACTIVE: the
   mandatory gate ran on every propose_rule (6/6) and caught 0/6 -- a genuinely
   novel rule proceeds unchanged.
3. **rep04 conflict-gate non-determinism is now harmless.** The S14 rep that
   returned blocked-then-compatible on the same text still wobbles (flagged
   R-PROMOTION-IMMUTABLE), but the mandatory gate demotes the restatement before
   the conflict gate's ambiguity can matter. On the genuinely novel new_rule
   proposals that DID reach the conflict gate, it was stable (6/6 compatible).
4. **rep02 self-corrected.** After the mandatory gate demoted its misroute and
   told it "this restates R-CONFIRM-VERSION," the model filed file_duplicate_rule
   as its actual route. The gate didn't just block a bad outcome; it handed the
   supervisor the right answer.
5. **Design principle demonstrated:** critical governance checks belong inside
   the institutional mechanism, not in the supervisor's discretionary workflow.
   The authority-bearing transition (propose_rule) runs the duplicate check
   itself; the model may still call check_duplicate_rule for preliminary
   reasoning, but the write boundary does not trust that somebody remembered.
