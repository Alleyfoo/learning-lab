**Enrichment adds fields to a row, it does not replace the row.** The driving
source's fields lead every output; a definer-declared spec for one of them keeps
its `type: number` and moves into position. In the model, not the executor.

**Triage before asking.** A blocked referent is load-bearing only when it is one
side of a join the policy has not settled, or when the observer never measured
it. A measured field is settled either way. Judged on referents and
measurements, never on question wording.

### THE ANNOYANCE — and it is a task-language gap, not a bug

Look at `cost`: `318.750`, `1520.00`, `633.9375`. Decimal multiplication gives a
scale that is the SUM of the operand scales, so money comes out with three and
four decimal places. Nobody wants that on a timesheet report.

The inspector *raised* it. Its single UNKNOWN was:

> Whether the 'hours' value and 'hourly_rate' use compatible time units without
> any hidden rounding or conversion rules.

Triage suppressed it, and **triage was right by its own rule**: the enrichment
model has no rounding construct, so no answer could have changed the model or
its output. The system correctly declined to ask a question it could not act on.

That is the useful finding. The question was not noise — it was pointing at a
concept the task vocabulary does not have. The next change this workflow argues
for is a declared output scale or rounding policy on a computed column, forced
by a real job rather than anticipated. Note that the repo has an existing rule
this must respect: predicates may normalise, emitted values may not, and a
computed value's representation is whatever the arithmetic produced (PRO-2
instance 9). A rounding policy would be a DECLARED change, not a helper.
