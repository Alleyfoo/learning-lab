Learning Lab · experimental architecture

From messy company work to verified, reusable operations.

Learning Lab explores how people can describe real work in normal language, use an LLM to inspect data and clarify meaning, and then turn what is learned into explicit deterministic processes that can be reused, supervised and improved.

The LLM does not need to own the system. It can talk to people and talk to a verified operational model while deterministic software owns established meaning, authority, execution, refusal and trace.

The starting point

Companies already have the process. Much of it lives between the systems.

A real process may cross Excel files, ERP exports, product systems, webshop imports, emails, campaign plans, warehouse decisions and the knowledge of several people. The systems contain fields. People know why those fields need to be correct.

Learning Lab starts from that real work rather than asking the company to model everything up front.

The process is also the data mine.

Each useful task can reveal product identities, source ownership, pricing rules, availability, mappings, responsibilities, constraints and system limitations. Once verified, that knowledge can be reused instead of rediscovered in the next automation.

The boundary

Conversation can define work. Conversation is not authority.

LLM / conversational layer

Interpret unfamiliar material, ask load-bearing questions, investigate exceptions, explain state, propose process definitions and suggest improvements.

Learning Lab / deterministic layer

Preserve established facts, validate structural contracts, version definitions, enforce authority, execute repeatable operations, refuse ambiguity and record provenance.

A valid conversational proposal can enter modelling and preview. It does not silently become an established process or production permission.

How work becomes reusable

Compile once, run many.

Bring one real task.A reconciliation, recurring report, product-data check, upload preparation or another bounded operational problem.
Observe what can be measured.Headers, structures, uniqueness, coverage and relationships stay separate from inferred business meaning.
Ask only what changes meaning.What identifies the same product? Which source owns price? What result is wanted? What may actually be changed?
Create a structural definition.The LLM proposes explicit sources, task semantics, outputs and unresolved questions instead of authority-bearing prose.
Validate, preview and establish.Mechanical checks catch contradictions; human confirmation remains explicit where meaning or authority is load-bearing.
Run without rediscovery.Recurring work executes deterministically. The LLM returns for exceptions, investigation and change.
Modular contracts

Data, work, output, destination and authority are different things.

Source contract

What data is this and what stable business role does it play?

Task contract

What should happen: reconcile, enrich, aggregate, report, update, reserve or publish?

Output contract

What constitutes the result: report, proposed change set, upload artifact or another explicit output?

Destination + authority

Where may the result go, and what effects are actually permitted there?

Integration

Upload files are adapters, not the business truth.

The canonical result should describe the desired change independently of how a particular target system accepts it.

Canonical change set SKU 123 SalePrice 19.90 → 14.90 Availability Oct 04 → Oct 01 Campaign none → Guest Specials Then adapt it to: → Excel / CSV import → API → staging database → SFTP batch → reviewed manual upload

If a webshop later replaces an upload format with an API, product identity and campaign meaning should survive. Replace and reverify the adapter rather than rediscovering the business process.

A real example

Campaign readiness is already a vertical slice of the idea.

The earlier Campaign Readiness Monitor prototype already models a useful distinction: campaign plan is intention, system configuration is current reality, and operations is the gap between them.

It checks planned products against price, dates, channel, content, stock and ownership data and turns mismatches into reviewable exceptions. Learning Lab generalizes the next step: can a company arrive at those definitions conversationally from unfamiliar real work, verify them once, and then reuse them across other processes?

Example: “Put these products on Guest Specials from 1–14 October.” The system can reuse product identity, campaign rules and mappings, discover that five products become available only on 4 October, and propose the availability correction as a prerequisite rather than merely reporting a bad field.

The bigger model

A field may be the final projection of several people's commitments.

campaign period → campaign planning supply feasibility → purchasing warehouse readiness → warehouse local participation → market / sales system availability → product-data operation

Over time the company model can therefore become a graph of facts, decisions, owners, dependencies, constraints and effects — built incrementally from actual work.

Analysis and development

The same model can help explain results and suggest better work.

Because the system knows both intent and execution, “How did last month’s campaign go?” can distinguish weak demand from operational failures such as late availability or partial publication.

The LLM can also suggest hypotheses — for example, two products that are frequently bought together may be worth testing as a bundle — while the verified system checks feasibility and the human still owns commercial judgement.

The same structure used to make work reliable can make analysis and process development more meaningful.
What already exists

This is research, but it is not starting from a blank page.

Deterministic floor

Explicit task models, source/input contracts, versioned workers, deterministic execution, refusal, provenance and recovery machinery.

Conversational definition experiments

A local Qwen model in Goose has produced structural process proposals from bounded sample data; deterministic validation accepts valid proposals and refuses contradictory ones by name.

Campaign Readiness Monitor

A prior vertical prototype for campaign intention vs system reality, exception detection, ownership and handoff.

Still direction, not a claim

Company-wide dependency graphs, effect read-back across real systems, capability composition and commercial suggestion loops still need real-world evidence.

A useful real proof case

Start with one annoying process, not with “digital transformation.”

A good first case is bounded enough to understand but messy enough to contain genuine company meaning. Examples include recurring Excel/reporting work, reconciliation between systems, product/master-data validation, campaign readiness, or preparation of ERP/PIM/webshop imports.

The important second test is whether the next process can reuse something learned from the first one.

Seeking a partner company

Six-month Saranen F.E.C. proof case.

I am looking for a company interested in testing this architecture on a real operational process through the Tekoäly asiantuntijatyössä F.E.C. recruiting training programme.

The programme includes a six-month full-time training-contract period in the partner company alongside specialist training. This is not a free consulting offer: the company joins the Saranen programme and pays the programme fee; during the training-contract period the participant is not yet employed by the company.

The proof case would begin with one narrow process and the company’s existing tools. The aim is to understand it properly, automate the repeatable part safely, preserve what is learned, and then test whether that knowledge helps with a second piece of work.

A sensible starting case can remain read-only and use previews, synthetic or anonymized data before any real write authority is considered. Current programme terms and company pricing should be confirmed directly with Saranen.

North star

The LLM can be the interface without being the system.

HUMANS DO BUSINESS ↕ LLM MAKES THE PROCESS CONVERSATIONAL ↕ LEARNING LAB MAKES IT EXPLICIT AND TRUSTWORTHY ↕ DETERMINISTIC CAPABILITIES MAKE IT REPEATABLE ↕ ADAPTERS CONNECT IT TO REAL SYSTEMS ↕ VERIFIED HISTORY MAKES THE COMPANY MODEL BETTER
Do not model the whole company first and then automate it. Model the company as people automate real work.
