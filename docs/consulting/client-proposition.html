Learning Lab · experimental architecture

From messy company work to verified, reusable operations.

Learning Lab explores a simple but ambitious idea: let people describe real work in normal language, let an LLM help understand the data and ask the missing questions, and turn what is learned into explicit deterministic processes that can be reused, verified and improved over time.

The LLM does not need to own the system. It can be the conversational layer between people and a verified operational model, while deterministic software owns established meaning, authority, execution and trace.

The problem

Companies already have processes. Much of the real process lives between the systems.

A business process may cross Excel files, ERP exports, product systems, webshop imports, emails, campaign plans, warehouse decisions and the knowledge of several people. The technical systems contain fields. People know why those fields need to be correct.

Traditional automation often starts from the outside: document the process, build a workflow, and maintain that workflow when the world changes. Learning Lab starts from the work itself.

The process is also the data mine.

Each useful task can reveal a little more about product identities, source ownership, pricing, availability, campaign logic, responsibilities, mappings, constraints and system limitations. Verified knowledge can then be reused instead of rediscovered in every new automation.

The boundary

Conversation can define work. Conversation is not authority.

LLM / Work layer

  • inspect permitted material
  • interpret unfamiliar data
  • ask load-bearing business questions
  • explain exceptions and state
  • propose a process or change
  • compose established capabilities
  • suggest improvements and experiments

Learning Lab / deterministic layer

  • preserve established facts
  • validate structural contracts
  • version process definitions
  • hold effect authority
  • execute repeatable operations
  • refuse ambiguity instead of guessing
  • record provenance and verify effects
How work becomes reusable

Compile once, run many.

Bring a real task.Files, exports, a reporting need, an upload problem, a reconciliation, a campaign setup or another bounded operational task.
Observe the data mechanically.Structure, headers, relationships, coverage and other measurable facts stay separate from inferred business meaning.
Discuss only what actually needs human meaning.What identifies the same product? Which source owns price? What result is wanted? Which system may be changed?
Create a structural Work Definition.The LLM proposes explicit source roles, task semantics, outputs, unresolved questions and requested destination — not an authority-bearing paragraph.
Validate, preview and establish.Deterministic validators catch contradictions. Human confirmation remains explicit where business meaning or authority is load-bearing.
Run without rediscovering the task.Once established, recurring work executes deterministically. The LLM comes back for exceptions, investigation, explanation and change.
Modularity

Input, work, output, destination and authority are different things.

Source contract

What is this data? What stable role does it play? Which structural facts are established?

Task contract

What should happen? Reconcile, enrich, aggregate, report, update, reserve, publish or another task-specific operation.

Output contract

What constitutes the result? A discrepancy report, a summary, a proposed change set, an upload artifact or another explicit result.

Destination contract

Where can the result go? Review screen, file, API, ERP, PIM, webshop, database or another system-specific adapter.

Effect authority

What may actually be changed? Read-only, draft, selected fields, publish, commit or explicit refusal.

Verified effect

Did the target system really reach the desired state? “Upload accepted” is not the same as “business change confirmed.”

System integration

Upload files are adapters, not the business truth.

Many real systems do not want constant ad-hoc uploads. The canonical result should therefore describe the desired business change independently of transport.

Canonical Change Set ──────────────────────────────── SKU 123 SalePrice 19.90 → 14.90 Availability Oct 04 → Oct 01 Campaign none → Guest Specials SKU 456 ... Then adapt the same verified change to: → Excel / CSV import → API → staging database → SFTP batch → human-reviewed manual upload

If the webshop later replaces its upload format with an API, product identity, campaign meaning, availability rules and pricing knowledge should survive. Replace and reverify the adapter — not the whole conceptual process.

Example

A campaign request is really a chain of commitments.

“Put these products on Guest Specials from 1–14 October.”
Product identity — known
Market participation — known / confirmed
Campaign pricing capability — known
Webshop mapping — known
Warehouse readiness — 28 September
Availability dates — 5 products start 4 October

Useful answer: Five products cannot participate for the full campaign period. Warehouse readiness is already confirmed before launch, so the system availability dates are the blocking condition. A prerequisite availability update can be proposed before campaign setup.

Organizational model

A field can be the final projection of several people's decisions.

Product availability may involve campaign planning, European purchasing, warehouse readiness, local market participation, sales responsibility and local marketing before one date finally appears in a product system.

campaign period owned by campaign planning ↓ supply feasibility owned by purchasing ↓ warehouse readiness owned by warehouse ↓ local participation owned by market ↓ system availability date implemented by product-data operation

The long-term company model is therefore not just columns and schemas. It can become a graph of facts, decisions, owners, dependencies, constraints and effects.

Process development

System limitations should not quietly become company policy.

“We only run 20% mass discounts” may be a real commercial rule — or it may simply be the workaround created because individual prices are painful to maintain in the current system.

Business requirement

What the company actually wants to achieve.

Operating practice

What people currently do.

Technical limitation

What the destination system makes difficult.

Improvement opportunity

A repeated workaround can become a candidate new verified capability.

The same system that executes work can therefore expose where the process itself deserves development.

Analysis and improvement

The operational model gives analytics context.

Once the system knows what a campaign intended, what was really published, what failed, what was available and what later sold, “How did the campaign go?” becomes richer than a sales query.

Intent

Products, prices, dates, markets, flyer promises, expected availability.

Operation

What actually published, partial effects, exceptions, late availability.

Outcome

Units, sales, margin, sell-through, stock-outs, returns.

Next hypothesis

For example: two products are often bought together and may be worth testing as a bundle.

The same verified model used to make work reliable can also make analysis more meaningful.

DIRECTION
What a real proof case could look like

Start with one annoying process, not with “digital transformation.”

A useful real-world case should be bounded enough to understand but messy enough to contain genuine company meaning. The goal would be to prove value on one process and preserve everything learned so a second process can reuse it.

Data reconciliation

Two or more sources that should agree but currently require manual comparison.

Reporting

A recurring report that combines operational data and business rules from several places.

Product / master data

Validation, enrichment, mapping, campaign readiness or preparation of approved system updates.

Import preparation

An ERP, PIM or webshop upload currently assembled manually from multiple data sources.

Seeking a real business case

Six-month proof case — no consulting fee.

I am looking for a company interested in testing this idea on a real operational process as a proof case connected to my Saranen LLM training.

The collaboration would start with one narrow process and existing company tools. The aim is not to replace ERP, PIM, RPA or workflow platforms. It is to understand one real piece of work, make its meaning explicit, automate the repeatable parts safely, and see whether the resulting company knowledge can be reused in the next piece of work.

The proof case would be free for six months. The interesting return is evidence: does this architecture actually make real company work easier, safer and more reusable?

A sensible first case should avoid unnecessary production risk. Preview, review-only outputs and synthetic/anonymized data can be used before any real write authority is considered.

The north star

The LLM can be the interface without being the system.
